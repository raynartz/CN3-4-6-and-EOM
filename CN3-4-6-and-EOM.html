Findings

Using the simulator

Directions refer to the patient. Choose a gaze and select “Repeat selected gaze” to repeat that movement. Select “All gaze positions” for continuous sequential playback. Pause freezes the animation. Selecting another gaze or lesion restarts the movement while preserving the play/pause state.

To demonstrate right INO, select Right MLF lesion and Left gaze. To demonstrate left INO, select Left MLF lesion and Right gaze. Select both MLF lesions for bilateral INO. Abducting nystagmus is animated during the gaze-holding phase.

Model limitations

This is a qualitative teaching model. Movement amplitudes, resting deviations, torsion, and nystagmus timing are illustrative. Clinical findings vary with lesion severity, fixation, chronicity, and orbital mechanics.

Peripheral nerve selections illustrate complete motor palsies. Vertical rectus actions are emphasized in abduction and oblique vertical actions in adduction; these positions do not completely isolate a muscle. Normal movement requires coordinated muscle activity.

MLF selections illustrate severe impairment of ipsilateral adduction during conjugate gaze and contralateral abducting nystagmus. Clinical INO may produce slowed or partially restricted adduction. Convergence can be preserved, but is not tested here. Associated skew deviation and other central ocular motor abnormalities are not modeled. Isolated INO is displayed without ptosis, pupil dilation, or a resting horizontal deviation.

Combined deficits are schematic. Resting alignment for multiple peripheral nerve palsies is not quantitatively modeled. A peripheral nerve palsy can mask an INO sign. This model does not represent a CN VI nuclear lesion, conjugate gaze palsy, or one-and-a-half syndrome.

Ptosis and pupil involvement are optional so that eye movements remain visible. Pupil dilation is not present in every CN III palsy. Diplopic images, accommodation, compensatory head posture, and the head-tilt test are not simulated.
